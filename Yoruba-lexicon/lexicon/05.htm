Ewédú   [ēwédú] a nutritious, slimy green soup made from cooked and blended jute leaves.

n. Jute leaf soup. Mo jẹ ọbẹ̀ Ewédú pẹ̀lú ọ̀pọ̀lọpọ̀ ẹran I ate jute leaf soup with a lot of meat.

Èbìrìpò   Ebiripo is a traditional Ijebu Remo dish from Ogun State. It is a steamed cocoyam pudding, also eaten as a swallow.[èbìɹìk͡pò] 

n. Steamed Cocoyam pudding. Kò dá bí temi, àwọn ọ̀rẹ́ mi fẹ́ràn ẹbirípọ̀ Unlike myself, my friends love Ebiripo.

Ègbo àti ẹ̀wà   [èg͡bō àtī ɛ̀wà] a traditional Yoruba comfort dish from southwestern Nigeria, consisting of a mixture of cooked, broken corn (Egbo) and beans (Ewa).

n. broken mashed corn and beans. Arábinrin náà kò ta ẹ̀gbo àti ẹ̀wà mọ̀n-ọ̀n The woman stop selling Ẹ̀gbo àti ẹ̀wà.

Èsúrú   n. a specie of yam commonly eaten in Nigeria and known for its medicinal properties in treating diabetes.[èsúɹú]  Ọbẹ̀ ata ni a fí n jẹ Èsúrú. Bitter yam is eaten with pepper sauce.

Ẹ̀bà   A cooked starchy food made from casava flakes with hot water, eaten with soup.[ɛ̀bà] 

n. starchy cassava flakes. ríi dájú pé o tẹ ẹ̀bà pẹ̀lú ọbẹ̀ ẹ̀gúsí lónìí Make sure you prepare ẹ̀bà with ẹ̀gúsí soup today.

Ẹ̀gúsí   [ɛ̀gúsí] the protein-rich, oily seeds of certain West African melons or gourds,which are dried or ground for soup.

n. A soup made out of melon seed. Mo fẹ́ràn ẹ̀gúsí I love Ẹ̀gúsí.

Ẹ̀kọ   [ɛ̀kɔ̄] Eko is a smooth cornmeal pudding made from cooked maize or cornflour. It is a firm, jelly-like food usually eaten with soups, stews, or with milk and sugar.

n. Solid pap. Ní òwúrọ̀, mo maa n jẹ Ẹ̀kọ. For breakfast, I usually eat Ẹ̀kọ.

Ẹ̀wà àgọ̀nyìn   [ɛ̀wà àgɔ̃̀jĩ̀] Popular food consisting of very soft mashed beans served with spicy, dark red-black palm oil and pepper sauce.

n. Mashed beans & pepper sauce. Mo fẹràn láti jẹ ẹ̀wà àgọ̀yìn mi pẹ̀lú búrẹ́dì rírọ̀ I prefer to eat my ẹ̀wà àgọ̀yìn with soft bread.
