Ilá   n. [īlá] a popular, savoury western Nigerian soup, made from chopped or blended okra, known for its slimy texture, flavoured with palm oil, and different kinds of protein. Ọbẹ̀ ilá náà yọ̀ púpọ̀ The okra soup is very slimy.

Iṣu   n. [īʃū] Isu is the Yoruba word for yam, a starchy root vegetable that is a major staple food in Nigeria and West Africa. Isu pẹ̀lú ẹyin jẹ́ àjọṣe tí Ọlọ́run dá Yam with eggs is a match made in heaven.

Iyán   n. [ījã́] A starch food made by pounding/blending boiled yam. Mo gún iyán ní èní I prepared pounded yam today.

Ìkọ́kọrẹ́   n. [ìkɔ́kɔ̄ɹɛ́] Ikokore is a traditional dish from the Ijebu people of Ogun State. It is a rich and savory water yam pottage, known for its hearty and slightly slimy texture. Mi ò tíì jẹ Ìkọ́kọrẹ́ fún àkókò pípẹ́ I haven't eaten Ìkọ́kọrẹ́ in a ,long time.

Ìlasà   n. [ìlāsà] a traditional Yoruba soup, made from okra leaves, originating from Igbo-Ora in Oyo State. Ó ra Ọbẹ̀ Ìlasà ni ọ̀dọ̀ olóúnjẹ She bought okra leaf soup from the food vendor.

Ìmóyò   n. [ìmójò] Imoyo is a traditional Yoruba (Anago) fish stew that is thin and savory. It is made with fresh fish, tomatoes, peppers, onions, palm oil, and locust beans, and is usually served with rice, eko, or yam. Ìyá-ìyá mi se Ìmóyò pẹlu ẹja tútù ati ìrẹsì My grandmother prepared Ìmóyò with fresh fish and rice.

Ìpẹ̀tẹ̀   n. [ìk͡pɛ̀tɛ̀] A yam dish cooked down with vegetables and oil into a dense meal. Ìyá àgbà mi kọ́ mi bí a ṣe ń ṣe oúnjẹ ìpẹ̀tẹ̀ tó yẹ My grandmother taught me how to make a proper ìpẹ̀tẹ̀ dish.

ìpékeré   n. [ìk͡pékeɹé] Crunchy, deep-fried snacks made from thinly sliced unripe or ripe plantains. Èló ni ìkpékeré yín? How much is your ìkpékeré?

ìṣápá   [ìʃák͡pá] n. a traditional delicacy from Western Nigeria, made from white hibiscus flower and typically thickened with Egusi (melon seeds). Ọbẹ̀ ìṣápá ni wọ́n sè fún ìnáwó náà The Hibiscus flower soup was cooked for the ceremony.
